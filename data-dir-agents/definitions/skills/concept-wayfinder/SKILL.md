# Concept Wayfinder

Find the way together before building. The centre is the client's goals, genuine understanding and informed decisions—not a pipeline or ticket system. A decision map supports collaboration; it does not define it.

This is conceptual planning, not implementation authorization. Particular designs remain proposals until agreed. Sketches, diagrams and notes are welcome when they help understanding; product code, installs, commits and publication need applicable authorization.

## Orient from files

Read `../../../AGENTS.md` and `../../../PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md`, then the relevant intent, questions and source pointers. Use `../../../docs/WAYFINDING_PLAN.md` for this effort's destination and approach. Resolve paths relative to this skill directory.

Keep information, planning, concepts, evidence and decisions in their canonical filesystem homes. Goal text stays lean: outcome, brief completion condition and file pointers. Do not create a second tracker or duplicate the conceptual corpus.

## Working principles

- **CLARITY CLARITY CLARITY:** pairing, collaborating and delegating need clear goals, context and guidelines—not nitty-gritty instructions.
- **CCC:** Context, Communication and Collaboration. **ACT:** Allied, Cohesive, Trusted. These are the user's vocabulary, not invented scores or forced consensus.
- Actively seek proper understanding. Build on KNOWN information; investigate gaps rather than guess. Hypotheses can guide inquiry but remain labeled until checked.
- Preserve provenance and verification limits throughout the organisation. Distinguish user intent, source observations, interpretation, proposals, decisions and uncertainty. A confident summary or recorded claim does not prove understanding or correctness.
- The user is the client, primarily bringing goals and requirements. The assistant coordinates contributions and speaks for the overall effort, including disagreement and decisions needing the client. Do not make the client manage individual agents or restrict their participation.

## Find the way

### Agree the destination

Establish what the conceptual outcome must make clear and what is outside scope. Use already confirmed intent; ask focused questions only for genuinely unresolved choices. Do not invent client agreement.

### Explore broadly, then focus

Surface relevant concepts, relationships, alternatives and open decisions before diving into mechanisms. Use actual collaboration episodes and our current user–assistant–peer collaboration as a living prototype. Learn from misunderstandings and failures too; today's tools and topology are not automatically the future architecture.

### Keep a lightweight decision map

Use existing question/status documents as the map where possible. Give questions meaningful names, link their evidence and indicate dependencies only where useful. Keep unresolved areas visible without pretending the whole route is known.

The map is an index. Each decision's detailed rationale, provenance, authority and unresolved disagreement live in one relevant artifact. Avoid mandatory ticket schemas, labels, claim states, remote issues or a fixed number of decisions per session.

### Investigate and decide together

Delegate useful source gathering and comparison to capable helpers or a team lead working with a team. Supply clear goals, boundaries and evidence expectations; leave room for independent contribution. Keep conceptual direction and client dialogue with the spokesperson. Collect and assess results; do not turn user-designated peers into owned workers.

Read original sources before adopting claims. Record what was checked and what remains unverified. Human choices require real human participation; research findings do not silently become approved architecture.

Present concise recommendations and meaningful alternatives. Update the map when evidence or requirements change. Add detail only when it improves understanding or unblocks a decision; do not manufacture ceremony.

### Synthesize and check

Bring the concepts into a coherent blueprint traceable to the client's requirements and recorded decisions. Challenge it against real collaboration experiences and the core principles. Make remaining uncertainty explicit; do not declare the route clear while consequential decisions remain open.

Moving to implementation planning or construction is a separate scope decision. Before finishing meaningful work, persist useful findings in their canonical homes and correct stale pointers. If persistence is blocked, report **not persisted**.
